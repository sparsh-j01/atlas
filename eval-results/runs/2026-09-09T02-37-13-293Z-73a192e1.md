### What Gate 3 licenses you to claim

**Gate 3 FAILED, and the failure is load-bearing — do not skim past it.**

Vector-only unique wins: **2**. BM25-only unique wins: **0**.

BM25 found nothing the dense arm did not already find. Fusing a ranker that contributes no
unique hits cannot add ranking information, so on THIS corpus hybrid retrieval is insurance,
not lift.

**The measured deltas say the same thing.** Hybrid MRR 0.920 vs vector-only 0.917
— a difference of +0.003. Recall@8: 100.0% vs 100.0%
(+0.0%).

**Say this, not "hybrid retrieval was necessary":**

> Dense retrieval saturates this corpus — Recall@8 is 100.0% for vector alone. BM25
> contributed no unique wins, so Gate 3 failed and is reported as failed. RRF fusion costs
> nothing measurable and covers the vocabulary-mismatch case the corpus happens not to
> contain, so it stays in — as insurance, not as demonstrated lift.

That is a stronger answer than a manufactured win: it names the limit of the evidence and
the engineering reason the component survives anyway. The honest follow-up is that a corpus
with rarer surface forms (acronyms, identifiers, case citations) is where BM25 earns its
place, and this corpus does not have enough of them — `patent-enforcement` came closest
(BM25 recall@10 84.6%) and is the model for a v2 golden set.
